# Customer Analytics and RFM Segmentation

An end-to-end data analytics project using SQL, Python, and Power BI to analyze customer purchasing behavior, identify valuable customer segments, and support data-driven retention strategies.

## Project Overview

This project analyzes transactional data from an online retail business to understand customer value, purchasing patterns, and retention.

The analysis uses the Online Retail II dataset to build a customer segmentation framework based on Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) analysis. The results are used to identify high-value customers, customers who may be at risk of churning, and opportunities for targeted retention strategies.

The project covers the complete analytics workflow, from data cleaning and validation to customer segmentation and interactive dashboard development.

## Business Problem

The business lacks a structured understanding of customer value and purchasing activity. Without customer segmentation, retention efforts may not effectively target valuable or inactive customers.

The objective is to analyze historical transactions and answer the following questions:

- Who are the most valuable customers, and how much revenue do they contribute?
- Which customers have become inactive and may be at risk of churning?
- How do customer value and purchasing patterns change over time?
- How does customer behavior vary across geographic markets?
- What retention strategies could be considered for different customer segments?

## Dataset

**Online Retail II — UCI Machine Learning Repository**

The dataset contains transaction records from a UK-based online retailer selling giftware and other products. It covers December 2009 to December 2011 and includes more than one million transaction records across two data sheets.

Key fields include:

- Invoice number
- Product code and description
- Quantity purchased
- Invoice date
- Unit price
- Customer identifier
- Country

Dataset: [UCI Online Retail II](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii)

## Project Workflow

### 1. Business Requirements

Defined the business problem, analytical questions, and key metrics before beginning the data analysis.

### 2. SQL Data Cleaning and Exploration

- Combined the two transaction sheets into a unified dataset.
- Handled inconsistent date formats and data type conversions.
- Identified and removed duplicate transactions.
- Examined invalid transaction records and missing customer identifiers.
- Validated cleaned data using row counts and consistency checks.

### 3. Python RFM Analysis

Calculated customer-level metrics using transaction history:

- **Recency:** Number of days since the customer's last purchase.
- **Frequency:** Number of distinct purchases made by the customer.
- **Monetary:** Total purchase value generated by the customer.

Customers were scored from 1 to 5 across the three dimensions and grouped into six segments:

- Champions
- Loyal Customers
- At Risk
- New Customers
- Needs Attention
- Lost Customers

A 90-day inactivity threshold was also used to flag customers who had not purchased recently.

### 4. Power BI Dashboard

The customer segmentation results were integrated with the cleaned transaction data to create an analysis-ready dataset for Power BI.

The dashboard contains three pages:

**Home**
- Project overview and navigation
- Definitions of RFM metrics and churn

**Business Overview**
- Total customers and revenue
- Customer churn percentage
- Average customer value
- Revenue and customer distribution by segment
- Monthly, quarterly, and yearly trends
- Filters for country, segment, time period, and churn status

**RFM Analysis**
- Customer count and revenue by segment
- Average RFM scores across segments
- Average customer value comparison
- Customer churn trends over time

## Key Findings

The analysis highlights the concentration of revenue among high-value customers and the importance of customer retention.

Reported results from the project analysis:

- Champions account for approximately 22% of customers and contribute close to 68% of revenue.
- The overall churn rate is approximately 50.86%, using a 90-day inactivity threshold.
- Customer value differs considerably across RFM segments.
- Revenue exhibits seasonal increases during the fourth quarter.
